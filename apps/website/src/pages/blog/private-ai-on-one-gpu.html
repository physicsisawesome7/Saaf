Running a private AI assistant on one GPU

Lalit Shukla
3 Nov 2026 · Guide
Self-hostingInferenceGPU

Many organisations can't send their documents to a cloud model, and can't wait for a data-centre budget either. A single consumer graphics card, used carefully, goes much further than most people expect.

Why one GPU

A card with 12 to 24 GB of memory costs about as much as a laptop and sits under a desk or in an existing server. Paired with open-weight models, it means questions, documents and answers never leave the building. The constraint is memory, so the job is to spend it well.

Where the memory goes

  • Model weights. Quantised to 4 bits (for example AWQ), a model needs roughly half a gigabyte per billion parameters, plus some overhead.
  • The KV cache. As the model reads a prompt and writes an answer, it keeps a cache for every token in flight. This decides how many questions can run at once and how long they can be. Storing it in 8-bit (FP8) on recent cards roughly doubles what fits.
  • Embeddings and reranking. Small models, a few hundred megabytes each. On a small card they can run on the CPU instead.

What fits

Smriti's installer picks a profile from the card it finds. As a guide: a 6 GB laptop card runs a 4-billion-parameter model with encoders on the CPU; 16 GB runs an 8B model with an FP8 cache; 24 GB runs a 14B model. A machine with no GPU still answers with verified extracts. The profile files show the arithmetic, so you can adjust it.

The biggest saving: don't call the model

The cheapest model call is the one you don't make. Many questions to an organisation's memory don't need a writer at all:

  • Lookups, such as "what does clause 16.1 say?", are answered with a verified quote.
  • Table questions, such as "what is the total?", are computed in code from the source rows.
  • Repeat questions from people with the same access reuse an answer that was already verified, through a guarded cache.
  • Attacks and unanswerable questions are stopped or abstained on before generation.

On Smriti's small demo question set, half the questions never reach the model. Your share will differ, which is why the next section matters.

The other tricks that matter

  • Short prompts. Cut passages to their relevant sentences, under a fixed token budget, however long the source documents are.
  • A fixed prefix. Start every prompt with the same rules, so the model server caches that part instead of recomputing it.
  • No hidden reasoning tokens for retrieval answers. They are slow and unnecessary when the answer is in the passages.
  • Admission control. When the card is saturated, serve the verified extract instead of letting requests queue until they time out.
  • Continuous batching and paged caches, which modern servers such as vLLM do for you.

Measure, don't guess

Throughput depends on your documents, your questions and your card. Run a load test on your own hardware before you size anything. Smriti ships one: smriti bench reports p50, p95 and p99 latency, answers per second, the share of answers that needed the model, and GPU tokens per answer.
