How it works

Mask on the way out. Restore on the way back.

Saaf sits between your users and any LLM, as a library, a gateway or a plugin. Real values stay in a short-lived vault on your side.

1

Detect

It cleans up hidden characters, lookalike letters and Indic digits, then checks every match with Verhoeff, Luhn and GSTIN maths.

2345 6789 0124 → Aadhaar ✓
2

Mask

Your policy decides whether to mask, block, warn or allow. Each value keeps the same token for the whole conversation.

[AADHAAR_1] goes to the model
3

Restore

Values come back even when the model changes case, drops brackets or streams the reply chunk by chunk.

[aadhaar_1]'s → 2345 6789 0124's

Features

        
Built for how Indian data really looks.

      

      

        
Checksums, not guesses
Any 12 digits match an "Aadhaar regex". Saaf checks the maths, and the nearest label wins, so "Order ID 2345…" stays untouched.

        
Hinglish & Indic scripts
"mera adhaar", आधार, and digits in Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu and five more scripts.

        
OCR repair
"2345 67B9 O124" from a scanned card is still an Aadhaar. The checksum confirms the repair before it counts.

        
Streaming-safe restore
Only the few characters that could start a token are held back. Everything else streams straight through.

        
Shadow mode
Watch what's really being sent for two weeks before you enforce anything. Types and counts only.

        
Signed audit & evidence
Hash-chained, signed records and an evidence pack (data flows, processors, retention) for your reviewers.

Security & privacy

        
Built so we never see your data.

        
Saaf runs on your infrastructure. Safearly never receives your prompts, replies or token mappings.

      

      

        

          
DataWhere it livesHow long

          
            
Your promptYour own process, in memoryFor the request

            
Token ↔ value vaultIn memory by default; optionally SQLite or Redis, encrypted with AES-GCMSession, or a TTL you set

            
What the LLM receivesTokens such as [PAN_1] in place of valuesThe provider's terms

            
Audit recordsYour log sink: types and counts only, signed and hash-chainedYour retention policy

            
Anything sent to SafearlyNothing. No telemetry.n/a

          
        

      

      

        
Careful by default
Persistent vaults refuse to run without an encryption key. Destroying a key crypto-shreds its data. Values never appear in logs.

        
Verifiable releases
Signed packages and container images, an SBOM with every release, a public threat model, and an external review of the vault cryptography before enterprise availability.

        
Honest limits
Saaf can miss names without an honorific, free-text addresses, images and unlabelled numbers. Full list.

      

      
Saaf provides evidence and safeguards for your own DPDP work. It is not a compliance certificate, and we will never claim it is. Found a vulnerability? Email lalit@safearly.com (we acknowledge within 3 working days).

FAQ
Questions, answered.

      

        
Does our data leave our network?
No. Saaf runs as a library, a gateway or a plugin on your own infrastructure. Only tokens reach the model provider, and Safearly never receives your traffic.

        
Is Saaf "DPDP compliant"?
No tool can make you compliant on its own. Saaf reduces how much personal data reaches LLMs and produces evidence (policies, signed audit logs, an evidence pack) for your own DPDP programme. Have your counsel review how you use it.

        
How is it different from a regex?
It validates checksums (Verhoeff, Luhn, GSTIN), scores the surrounding words, repairs OCR errors and normalises Unicode. On look-alike numbers, a bare regex raised 236.95 false alarms per 1,000 tokens; Saaf raised 0.83.

        
Does it work with streaming responses?
Yes. Text streams through as it arrives; only the few characters that could start a token are held back. Tests check that any chunking gives the same result.

        
What doesn't it catch?
Names without an honorific, free-text addresses, images, and unlabelled generic numbers such as bank accounts or PIN codes. An Indic name-recognition model is on the roadmap.

        
What does this website collect?
Nothing. There are no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts. The demo runs entirely in your browser. If you email us, we use your address only to reply.
